## B. Online commerce in Albania

### B1. Individuals and households (INSTAT ICT survey 2025, ages 16–74; published 11/12/2025)
Source: https://www.instat.gov.al/media/4jkhqz0c/ict-2025_english.pdf (release page: https://www.instat.gov.al/en/themes/social-condition/information-and-communication-technologies-ict-usage-in-households-and-by-individuals/publication/2025/survey-on-information-and-communication-technologies-ict-usage-in-households-and-by-individuals-in-2025/)
- **87.2 %** used the internet in the last 3 months. Of these, 97.3 % use it several times a day.
- **97.7 %** of households have internet access: 92.1 % fixed broadband, 99.3 % mobile broadband.
- 99.9 % of internet users go online by mobile/smartphone. Only 41.2 % use a laptop and 20.9 % a desktop. **The site must be designed for mobile first.**
- **45.4 %** bought something online in the last 12 months (+~3.0 p.p.). 26.1 % bought online in the last 3 months (+5.7 p.p.).
- Main categories bought online (share of recent online buyers, 2025): clothes/shoes 96.5 %, restaurant delivery 61.1 %, cosmetics 45.3 %, food 44.6 %, sport goods 34.3 %, household products 26.6 %, **consumer electronics/household appliances 20.2 %**. There is no construction or energy category in the survey.
- 99.5 % have an e-Albania account. People are used to digital identity and online government services.
- Next edition (2026) is pending.

### B2. Enterprises (INSTAT ICT in enterprises 2026, firms with 10+ employees; published 21/09/2026)
Source: https://www.instat.gov.al/media/bfbjhpvm/ict-in-interprises-2026.pdf
- 62.9 % of enterprises have a website (61.0 % in 2025).

### B3. Cards and electronic payments (Bank of Albania)
Source: Bank of Albania *Annual Report 2025*, ch. 2.7, pp. 83–85, https://www.bankofalbania.org/rc/doc/RV_2025_Anglisht_2026_35084.pdf
- **1,652,474 active cards** at the end of 2025 (+9.4 %). Of these, 88.1 % are debit, 7.3 % credit and 4.6 % e-money cards.
- Card payments made up about 67 % of the number of payments through banks. The number of card payments rose 27.5 %, and 94 % of them are made by households.
- Card purchases at POS rose 38.7 % in number and 26.9 % in value. "For the first time in 2025, cards were used primarily to conduct payments", not mainly for cash withdrawals.
- The number of **virtual POS** (e-commerce acquiring) rose **22.1 %** in 2025. The BoA says this indicates "that e-commerce has become increasingly prominent". It does not publish an absolute count.
- Physical POS terminals rose 27.7 %.
- 53.2 million electronic payments went through banks (+25.6 %), worth ALL 3,187 bn. The report prints this as "ALL 53.2 million", which is a typo for the number of payments. That is **29 e-payments per capita** (age 15+).
- Home-banking transactions rose 23.2 %. Remotely accessible accounts rose 44.4 % to 46.8 % of all accounts.
- 2024 baseline (BoA Annual Report 2024, payment systems chapter, https://www.bankofalbania.org/rc/doc/Payment_systems_2024_anglisht_32361.pdf): 1,510,606 active cards; average debit-card transaction ALL 6,746. **On 21 Nov 2024 the European Payments Council approved Albania's inclusion in the SEPA scheme.** EUR transfers to and from EU suppliers get easier.
- Banks: the BoA lists **12 banks + 1 foreign-bank branch**. These are American Bank of Investment, Credins, First Investment Bank, Intesa Sanpaolo, Jet Bank, National Commercial Bank (BKT), OTP Bank Albania, ProCredit, Raiffeisen, Tirana Bank, Union Bank, United Bank of Albania, and Ziraat (branch). Source: https://www.bankofalbania.org/Supervision/Licensed_institutions/Banks/. The Annual Report 2025 says "all the eleven banks … are licensed as card issuers, of which nine … as card accepters". ## C. Legal requirements for an online shop in Albania

### C1. E-commerce law — Law 10128/2009 "Për tregtinë elektronike", amended by Law 135/2013
Consolidated text (hosted by the IDP): https://idp.al/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/ligji_10128_per_tregtine_elektronike_i_ndryshuar.pdf. It is aligned with Directive 2000/31/EC. I did not check whether there were amendments after 2013.
- **Art. 7 — general information the site must show:** trade name; registered seat address; e-mail or other quick contact; business-registry data, "sidomos numrin unik të identifikimit"; supervisory authority if the activity needs a licence; **NIPT** (art. 7.1.e). Prices must be clear and state that they are **final, including tax and delivery costs** (art. 7.2).
- **Art. 8 — commercial communications:** must be identifiable as commercial and name the sender. Promotions must state their conditions clearly.
- **Art. 9 — unsolicited e-mail or SMS marketing only with prior consent.** The sender must keep an opt-out register ("regjistër përjashtimesh").
- **Art. 13 — before the order, inform the buyer of:** the technical steps to conclude the contract; the contract terms; how to spot and correct input errors before ordering; the languages offered; and whether the contract is stored and accessible. Terms must be downloadable and storable (13.3).
- **Art. 14 — confirm receipt of the order without delay by electronic means.**
- **Art. 17 — hosting safe harbour.** A platform storing third-party content (for us, seller listings) is not liable if it had no knowledge, and it must act promptly to remove illegal content once aware. This applies only if the seller does not act under the platform's control.
- **Art. 20.2 — duty to notify authorities of suspected illegal activity.**
- **Art. 21:** enforced by the Consumer Protection Commission (Komisioni i Mbrojtjes së Konsumatorëve).
- **Art. 22 — fines:** ALL 200,000 for a legal person (ALL 100,000 for a natural person) for breaching arts. 7, 8, 9, 13.1, 13.3 and others.

**Law 50/2025** ratified CEFTA Joint Committee Decision No. 2/2024 "On the Facilitation of Electronic Commerce". PwC Albania summarises it as: e-contracts are valid; clear consumer information; a **14-day withdrawal right for distance sales** (including digital content); transparency and fairness duties for **online platforms**; limited intermediary liability; dispute resolution. It says businesses "must update terms, consumer information, and internal processes". Source: PwC Albania Legal Alert, Aug 2025, https://www.pwc.com/al/en/Legal%20Alert%20-%20August%202025.pdf. **Secondary source** — I did not read the gazette text of Law 50/2025.

### C2. Consumer protection — Law 9902/2008 "Për mbrojtjen e konsumatorëve", as amended (incl. Law 71/2018)
Consolidated 2018 text hosted by the consumer authority: https://aac.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Ligji-9902-date-17.04.2008-i-ndryshuar-version-i-konsoliduar-2018.doc. Law 71/2018 amending it: https://erru.al/doc/Ligji_nr_71-2018_date_18.10.2018_Per_disa_shtesa_dhe_ndryshime_ne_ligjin_nr_9902_date_17.04.2008.pdf. A search on 2026-10-23 found **no new consumer-protection law** replacing 9902. That absence is **UNVERIFIED** against the Kuvendi website and the official gazette.

Rules that shape the shop:
- **Art. 9 — price display:** every product shows its selling price. Packaged goods also show a **unit price** (per kg, l, m, m², m³ — art. 3 defines unit price as the final price incl. VAT). **Art. 9.4: "Çmimi i shitjes … shprehet vetëm në lekë"** — the price is expressed only in lek. **→ The consumer price must be in ALL.** Showing EUR alongside as a reference is a legal grey zone; confirm with a lawyer.
- **Albanian language:** product labelling (art. 8), essential product and service information (art. 12: "Të gjitha të dhënat dhe informacionet më të domosdoshme … duhet të jenë në gjuhën shqipe"), advertising messages (art. 22), written consumer contract terms (art. 28) and warranty statements (art. 32.3) must be in Albanian. The English version is an addition, not a replacement.
- **Art. 36 — pre-contract information for distance contracts:** main characteristics of the goods; registered name; postal address, phone and e-mail; complaints address; **full price incl. taxes** plus delivery costs; payment, delivery and complaint-handling arrangements and the delivery date; the withdrawal right with its conditions, deadline and procedure plus the **model withdrawal form (Annex I(B))**; who pays return costs (and the cost for goods that cannot go by post — relevant for panels and pallets); legal guarantee; after-sales service.
- **Art. 37.2 — "button rule":** the order button must clearly say the order involves an obligation to pay ("porosi me detyrim pagese" or an equally clear wording). Immediately before the order, show the main characteristics, total price, contract duration and minimum obligations.
- **Art. 37.3:** at the start of checkout, state any **delivery restrictions** and the **accepted payment methods**.
- **Art. 37.7:** confirm the contract on a durable medium (e-mail) no later than delivery.
- **Art. 37/1 — withdrawal right: 14 days**, no reason needed. For goods it runs from the day the consumer takes possession (for split deliveries, from the last item).
- **Art. 37/2:** if the trader omits the withdrawal information, the period extends by up to 12 months.
- **Art. 37/3:** the consumer may use the model form or any clear statement. If the site offers an online withdrawal form, it must acknowledge receipt without delay on a durable medium.
- **Art. 37/5:** refund all payments **including the standard delivery cost within 14 days** of the withdrawal notice.
- **Art. 37/6:** the consumer returns the goods within 14 days and is liable only for diminished value from handling beyond normal inspection.
- **Art. 37/8 — exceptions:** goods **made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalised** (e.g. cut-to-size materials, custom windows); perishables; sealed goods unsuitable for return for health reasons; and others. Mark such products in the admin.
- **Art. 37/10 — delivery:** unless agreed otherwise, deliver **no later than 30 days** after the contract.
- **Art. 37/11:** surcharges for a payment method may not exceed the trader's actual cost.
- **Art. 37/12:** risk passes on physical possession by the consumer.
- **Art. 37/14:** additional paid extras need **express consent** — no pre-ticked boxes.
- **Art. 30 — legal guarantee of conformity: 2 years from delivery.** Defects appearing within 6 months are presumed to have existed at delivery. **Art. 31:** repair or replacement within a reasonable period, no more than 30 calendar days.
- **Art. 29.4:** faulty installation counts as non-conformity when installation is part of the sales contract, or when the consumer installed the goods badly because of faulty instructions. This matters if sellers offer installation.
- **Fines (art. 57):** ALL 100,000 for breaching arts. 9, 11 and 18; **ALL 200,000** for breaching arts. 7, 8, 9(1–2), 12, 30, 31, 32 and 33. Imposed by the market-surveillance inspectorate; the Consumer Protection Commission has its own sanctions.
- **Conflict note:** a 2018 KMK leaflet (https://kmk.ekonomia.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/FP-informuese-per-kontratat-ne-largesi.pdf) says goods are returned within **5 days** and refunds paid within **15 days**. The consolidated law says **14 / 14 days**. **The law text is the stronger source; use 14/14.** The leaflet does agree on the 14-day withdrawal period and a 30-day maximum delivery.

### C3. Personal data — Law 124/2024 "Për mbrojtjen e të dhënave personale"
Text: https://idp.al/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ligj-2024-12-19-124.pdf (listed at https://idp.al/legjislacion-per-mbrojtjen-e-te-dhenave-personale/). It replaces Law 9887/2008 and is fully aligned with GDPR (EU) 2016/679 (footnote 1).
- Adopted 19.12.2024; promulgated by decree no. 5, 15.1.2025. It enters into force **15 days after publication** in the Official Gazette (art. 101.1). The gazette issue number and date are **UNVERIFIED**.
- Delayed provisions: arts. 29(3), 31 (DPIA), 32 (prior consultation), 35 (codes of conduct), 36 (monitoring bodies), 64, 65 and 67(2, 3, 5) apply **2 years after publication** (art. 101.2) — around early 2027 (calc.).
- Lawful bases as in GDPR (art. 7): consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interest, public task, legitimate interest.
- **Children:** online-service consent is valid only from age **16**. Under 16, a parent must consent (art. 8.6).
- **Breach notification to the Commissioner within 72 hours** (art. 29.1).
- **Data Protection Officer** (art. 33): mandatory when the core activity is large-scale regular and systematic monitoring, or large-scale sensitive data. A marketplace may or may not fall under this — **ask counsel**. The Commissioner keeps an electronic register of DPOs (DCM 347/2025, on the same IDP page).
- Direct marketing has a dedicated article, **art. 46** ("Përpunimi i të dhënave për qëllime të marketingut të drejtpërdrejtë"). The data subject has a right to object to direct-marketing processing at any time (**art. 19.2**, "E drejta për të kundërshtuar").
- **Fines** (art. 94): up to ALL 1 bn or 2 % of global turnover for controller/processor obligations; up to **ALL 2 bn or 4 %** for breaching principles, consent conditions (arts. 6–9) or data-subject rights (arts. 12–20), whichever is higher.
- **Cookies:** Law 124/2024 contains **no cookie-specific article** (text searched for "cookie" and "pajisje terminale"; no hits). The ePrivacy-type rule would sit in electronic-communications law (Law 54/2024). I did not read it, because the IDP link labelled 54/2024 actually serves the old Law 9918/2008. Cookie-consent obligation = **UNVERIFIED**. Practice: the Commissioner's own site (idp.al) runs a cookie banner with "Cookies rreptësisht të nevojshme" and on/off settings. A GDPR-style opt-in banner (no non-essential cookies before consent) is the safe choice.

### C4. What must appear on the site (collected from C1–C3 and BKT's e-commerce merchant rules)
BKT's Virtual POS page (https://www.bkt.com.al/en/business/daily-operations/virtual-pos-services/virtual-pos) lists what an acquiring bank checks before approving a webshop. The checks are: correct company information (trade name, country, address); customer-service phone and e-mail; product prices and details; **cancellation/return policy, distance-sales contract or service agreement, general terms, place of jurisdiction, and delivery terms**; a catalogue and cart; card-scheme logos; an SSL certificate; and **the exchange rate if prices are shown in a foreign currency**. Combined with the laws:
1. Legal name (sh.p.k.), **NIPT**, registered address, e-mail and phone (law 10128 art. 7; law 9902 art. 36).
2. Prices final, incl. **TVSH 20 %**, in **lek**, with unit price where relevant (per m², per kg, per Wp is a useful extra). Delivery cost shown before the order.
3. Terms of use, distance-sales terms, **withdrawal/return policy with the model withdrawal form**, warranty info (2-year legal guarantee), delivery restrictions and payment methods, complaint procedure.
4. Privacy policy and cookie notice (Law 124/2024).
5. An order button labelled as a payment obligation, an order summary, an error-correction step, and an e-mail confirmation.
6. Albanian-language versions of all consumer-facing legal text.

## D. Tax

### D1. VAT (TVSH)
Source: PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries — Albania, other taxes, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/albania/corporate/other-taxes (read via reader proxy). Underlying law: VAT Law 92/2014 (not read directly).
- **Standard rate 20 %.** Monthly VAT period.
- Reduced **6 %**: accommodation, certified agritourism, advertising by audio-visual media, books, electric public-transport buses (9+ seats).
- Reduced **10 %**: agricultural inputs (fertilisers, pesticides, seeds, seedlings).
- Exempt items include **new zero-mileage electric cars** not previously registered abroad, agricultural machines and postal services.
- **No reduced rate or exemption for solar panels, inverters, batteries, heat pumps or building materials** appears in the PwC list. A search in Albanian found none either. A 2024 public solar procurement estimate applies 20 % TVSH (https://openprocurement.al/fund/preventiv/1734947669920prv.pdf). → Treat as **20 %**; formal confirmation from the VAT law or an adviser is still **UNVERIFIED**.
- VAT registration threshold: annual turnover **ALL 10 million**.
- VAT books are due on the 11th and the VAT return and payment on the 14th of the following month, all electronically.
- Customs: 0–15 %. Machinery and equipment imported for business use are generally at 0 %.

### D2. Fiscalization — Law 87/2019 "Për faturën dhe sistemin e monitorimit të qarkullimit"
Implementing instruction: Instruction No. 16, 3.4.2020 (consolidated with amendments up to Instr. 13 of 9.5.2023), https://qeverisjavendore.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Udhezim-nr.-16-date-3.4.-2020-Per-faturen-dhe-sistemin-e-monitorimit-te-qarkullimit.pdf. The law text itself (tatime.gov.al/shkarko.php?id=8068) did not load.
- **Every invoice is fiscalized in real time** through the central invoice platform (built by AKSHI, run by the tax administration). Access uses an AKSHI-issued digital certificate (Instr. 16, art. 31).
- Mandatory invoice elements include the **NIVF** (unique invoice identification number, 32 characters, returned by the tax system after verification), the **NSLF** (issuer security code, generated by the seller's software) and a **QR code** (Instr. 16 arts. 9–12).
- **Payment-type split (Instr. 16 art. 9.8): card payments (credit/debit) count as "pagesa me para në dorë" (cash-type)**, along with banknotes, cheques and vouchers. Bank-account transfers, factoring, compensation and similar are "pa para në dorë" (non-cash). One invoice cannot mix cash-type and non-cash payments. If the seller sends a payment order the customer pays by e-banking, the invoice is non-cash.
- **Online shops:** art. 29.1 says that for goods or services ordered through **an app or internet shop** the invoice need not be printed — it may be sent by e-mail or as an e-invoice. Example given: a distance sale paid by the consumer's credit card is a cash-type payment, and the invoice can be e-mailed as a PDF.
- Non-cash B2C invoices can be on paper or e-mail, with the buyer's consent (art. 29.2).
- Online shops may declare business hours **00:00–24:00** (Instr. 16, place-of-business registration).
- A seller accepting only cards (e.g. a webshop) does **not** have to register a daily cash deposit (art. 40.7).
- **E-invoice (B2B / B2G) — Instr. 16 art. 30:** an electronic invoice in the Albanian standard format (SSH EN 16931-1) is mandatory **(a) when both seller and buyer are taxpayers and the invoice is paid non-cash**, and **(b) whenever a public body is the buyer**. A PDF is not an e-invoice. → B2B checkout must capture the **buyer's NIPT** and offer bank transfer, and the seller's software must issue an e-invoice.
- Phase-in dates (B2G from 1 Jan 2021, B2B from 1 Jul 2021, cash from 1 Sep 2021, per Law 108/2020) come from a Deloitte Albania July 2020 newsletter cited in search results. That link now returns 404 → **UNVERIFIED** in detail. The system is clearly in force: a tatime.gov.al notice on the fiscalization system's operation was live on 2026-10-23.
- **New: POS/POI deadline.** Tax Directorate notice dated 21.09.2026 (https://tatime.gov.al/pajisja-me-pos-poi-31-dhjetor-2026-afati-i-fundit/): taxpayers making cash sales must have a **POS/POI card terminal at every point of sale by 31 December 2026** (Law 9920/2008 art. 59/3). There are exemptions for areas without internet, invoice-exempt taxpayers, and certain non-VAT sole self-employed with one location. Whether this applies to online-only sellers is **UNVERIFIED** — ask an accountant.
- **Marketplace implication (inference, not legal advice):** the seller who supplies the goods issues the fiscal invoice (NIVF/NSLF/QR) from its own certified invoicing software. BeeD invoices sellers for subscriptions and commission. The platform should store or link each seller's fiscal invoice (NIVF, PDF) on the order. Confirm the model with a tax adviser before launch.

### D3. NIPT format
- **One letter + 8 digits + one letter, 10 characters** (e.g. `J61924007N` — Posta Shqiptare sh.a.'s NIPT as printed in the AKEP register, see F). The first letter encodes the decade of assignment (A–M are valid as the first letter). The last letter is a check letter whose algorithm is not public.
- Validation regex used by the open-source `python-stdnum` library: `^[A-M][0-9]{8}[A-Z]$`. Source: https://github.com/arthurdejong/python-stdnum/blob/master/stdnum/al/nipt.py (which cites tatime.gov.al).
- Registry examples seen in BoA and AKEP lists: `J61924007N`, `K61730019P`, `L62422006H`, `M21823001C`, `M52215035C` — all fit the pattern.
- This is a secondary/technical source, but consistent with every real NIPT seen. Accept the format check only; you cannot verify a check letter offline.
